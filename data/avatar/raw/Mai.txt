Quick Answers
Who are Mai's parents?
Mai's parents are Ukano and Michi. They come from a wealthy background and spoiled Mai as long as she behaved. However, Mai found little comfort in material things and craved excitement and freedom, leading to a rebellious attitude towards her parents.
Provided by: Community
What is Mai's preferred fighting style?
Mai was exceptionally proficient at throwing stilettos, a skill she taught herself out of boredom by throwing knives at her bedroom wall. She has small knives and spring-loaded holsters concealed in her robes, and was able to skillfully hit targets from a distance. With such deadly accuracy, Mai is able to pin her opponents down, even if they are moving, a useful sill against benders who often need their hands and arms free to bend.
Provided by: Community
What role does Mai play in Princess Azula's plans during the Hundred Year War?
Mai is initially recruited by her childhood friend Azula to hunt down and capture Prince Zuko, Iroh, and later Avatar Aang and his friends. However, Mai's loyalty to Zuko leads her to betray Azula. This act of defiance, coupled with Ty Lee's defense of Mai, results in their imprisonment, significantly impacting Azula's leadership and leading to the breakdown of her psyche.
Provided by: Community
What is Mai's status in the Fire Nation society?
Mai holds a high status in the Fire Nation society. She was born to Ukano and Michi, making her one of the most privileged girls in the Fire Nation. She attended the Royal Fire Academy for Girls and was a close friend of Princess Azula and Ty Lee. Mai was also Prince Zuko's love interest upon his return to the Fire Nation. Despite her high status, Mai's upbringing was strict, with her parents requiring her to behave perfectly, which led to her emotional distance in later years.
Provided by: Fandom
How does Mai's relationship with Zuko evolve throughout the series?
Mai's relationship with Zuko is a complex and evolving one. It starts with a childhood crush, which later blossoms into a romantic relationship when Zuko returns to the Fire Nation. They share strong feelings for each other, but their relationship isn't without its challenges. They temporarily break up during a rough patch on Ember Island due to Zuko's temper, but they soon reconcile after addressing their issues. Mai is deeply concerned about Zuko's internal conflict regarding his father and the war, and though she tries to make him feel more comfortable, she ultimately breaks up with him after he kept secrets rom her.

As a result of her calm attitude, Mai was not easily intimidated and she was one of the few people who did not openly showcase fear of Azula. Instead, she respected her great power, and initially stayed loyal to her due to feeling more important around Azula.2] For years, Azula gave Mai the feeling that her opinions mattered, something which she long lacked in interactions with her parents..2]3]
Nevertheless, Mai had always disliked Azula's manipulations, ruthless behavior, and callous disregard for others, though she did not dare to strongly oppose the princess' actions.2]3] Like Zuko, Mai was opposed to the idea of needless killing and unnecessary violence; and more often than not, she chose to disable foes rather than outright kill them during battle. She generally achieved this by using her weapons to pin their clothes, usually the sleeves, to a vertical surface.4] Having disdained the nationalistic and often hateful teachings of her alma mater, the Royal Fire Academy for Girls, Mai held a cynical view of the Fire Nation's imperialism during her youth.3] She did not display any bigotry toward people of other nations, attitudes that were characteristic of much of the Fire Nation nobility.4] As a result, she was an easy convert to the Fire Nation's reforms after Zuko's accession of the throne, viewing them as means to avoid future suffering and work toward a better future.3]
Despite her stoic nature, Mai's strong relationship with Zuko tended to bring out her more passionate side. Azula took note of Mai's "strangely good mood" since Zuko's return to the Fire Nation.5] When Azula started toying with the couple and mocked her boyfriend, Mai soothed him by assuring him that, even though she had trouble showing it, she did love him.1] She also expressed deep concern when he fretted over his father and the war meetings, and tried to make him feel more comfortable.6] She was devastated by his sudden departure from the Fire Nation and consequently harbored some resentment toward him, as evidenced by her bitter confrontation with him in his prison cell at the Boiling Rock. Despite her hurt, however, she retained a great affection for Zuko, aiding his escape from the prison and, in the process, betraying her loyalty to Azula; she later affirmed to the princess that she loved Zuko more than she feared the latter.7] Unwilling to hold a grudge, Mai happily reunited with Zuko after the Hundred Year War ended.8]
Following the War's end, Mai began to develop something of a nurturing streak, becoming more openly affectionate of her little brother Tom-Tom and eventually finding her calling as a teacher at the Royal Fire Academy for Girls. Mai's previous differences in opinion with traditional Fire Nation nobility of the time would also become much more prevalent post-war, as she was one of the staunchest supporters of the reforms Zuko wished to implement as Fire Lord, openly opining that the less of the Fire Nation's war era traditions remained, the better. In particular, she worked hard to welcome her students equally regardless of their socioeconomic status, ensure the true history of the Hundred Year War would be taught to them, and forbid the custom of Agni Kai duels as a means of settling disputes. She attributed the previous era's traditions with the Fire Nation's loss, as well as the failure of individuals such as Azula. As a teacher, Mai took a hands-on and improvisational approach to the job, such as allowing her students to catch bugs outdoors while teaching them about nature. She encouraged her students' curiosity, kindness, and respect that was not based on fear of retribution, but instead on the common humanity that all peoples share. Though she continued practicing her knife-throwing skills, she refused to take her weapons onto school grounds.3]

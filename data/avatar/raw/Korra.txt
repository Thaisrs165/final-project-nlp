Quick Answers
Who is Korra?
Korra was the Water Avatar succeeding Avatar Aang and the predecessor of Avatars Pavi and Nisha. She was born and raised in the Southern Water Tribe and later relocated to Republic City to master airbending under the guidance of Tenzin.
Provided by: Community
Who trained Korra in waterbending?
Korra was trained in waterbending by Katara, and later learnt the specialised technique of spiritbending from her uncle, Unalaq.
Provided by: Community
Who kidnapped Korra in 158 AG?
Korra was kidnapped by the Red Lotus in 158 AG, consisting of Zaheer, Ghazan, Ming-Hua, and P'Li.
Provided by: Community
How did Korra and Naga become close companions?
Korra found a lost polar bear pup and gained its trust after feeding it some fish jerky and playing together. As a storm broke out, the two sheltered together, keeping each other warm until Tonraq saved them. Allowed to keep Naga, they became inseparable during the following years growing up, with Naga becoming Korra's best friend, animal guide, and main means of transport.
Provided by: Community
Who cleared Korra of charges when she got in trouble with the Metalbending Police Force?
Tenzin intervened and cleared Korra of all charges.

On the surface, Korra was seemingly the opposite of her immediate predecessor Aang, being fierce, independent, stubborn, headstrong, and pugnacious. Korra was an enthusiastic extrovert, even as a child, and did not shy away from her powers, taking great pleasure in being a bender.2] As an Avatar, she slowly came into the role, initially believing that bending prowess was all that she needed to focus on.3]4] Over time, Korra began to take in how much responsibility she had to the world at large and the importance of keeping it balanced.5]6]
Beneath her tough exterior, she possessed a strong loyalty toward her friends and duty as the Avatar. She also had admirable compassion and devotion, demonstrated when she expressed love toward her family and friends.2]7] Her offensive battle style meant that she was always quick to initiate or rise to a challenge and was similarly quick-witted and impulsive, especially in heated situations. As an "Avatar-in-training", she lacked perception toward the damage her bending was capable of, and her rash decisions often resulted in her accidentally insulting others and getting herself in trouble.2]
True to Korra's character, Lin once stated to Tenzin that it was "hard to believe [his] sweet-tempered father was reincarnated into that girl".8] Her personality was, in many ways, the opposite of her past life's; Aang was a peaceful, diffident, carefree airbender, whereas Korra was tough, impatient, headstrong, and assertive. She had a habit of getting close-up to people when confronting them, sometimes grabbing them by the chin when trying to get her point across.9]10] Additionally, while Aang was initially reluctant to be the Avatar, to the point where he ran away to prevent his responsibilities as the Avatar from disturbing his life, Korra fully embraced her position as the Avatar from a young age reveling in its power. However, similar to Aang, she also had a good sense of humor, enthusiasm, and vulnerable teenage charm.11]
Being initially unable to connect with her spiritual side proved to be a stumbling block in Korra's training, and she admitted that her personality was the opposite of the typical airbender's; as Tenzin noted, Avatars tend to struggle with the bending art most opposite their personality.4] Due to the lack of a spiritual center in her life, Korra was impulsive and therefore had a hard time hiding her true feelings. She sometimes tended to let her judgment be run by her emotions, a flaw that got her into trouble numerous times, like when she eventually made the rash decision to call Amon out in a one-on-one duel. This indicates that she was ruled by her emotions even when she tried to pretend they did not exist. This could also be seen in her reaction to learning that Asami Sato was Mako's date during the gala Tarrlok threw in her honor, where she immediately expressed her jealousy by being rather hostile toward Asami and short with Mako.12]
Due to her concentration on the physical side of bending, Korra was initially quite worried when she saw "the revelation" of Amon's true abilities during his demonstration,13] a fear which expressed itself in Amon's words in the nightmare she had soon afterward, "Once I take your bending away, you will be nothing." This and her later encounter with Amon revealed this fear. She tried repressing it for the good of Republic City when she joined Tarrlok's task force but had trouble staying her usual upbeat self and became rather reclusive and eventually made the rash decision to call Amon out in a one-on-one duel.12]
Like her immediate predecessor Aang, Korra placed a high priority on her position as the Avatar, despite their different approaches. Although at first, Aang was uncomfortable with having to use outright violence to bring balance to the world, he eventually developed an unrelenting determination toward following his age-old destiny.14]15] Korra, on the other hand, took a non-conformist stance over her Avatar training. For instance, after trying out pro-bending, she told Tenzin that the reason she had been unable to airbend was that the Avatar no longer needed it and was instead meant to focus on modern styles of fighting. Tenzin quickly stamped out her proposal as preposterous, reminding her that the Avatar is destined to master all four elements at all costs.4]
Korra's sense of self-worth was deeply tied to her status as the Avatar, something that was frequently challenged by her enemies, such as Amon and Kuvira arguing that the modernized world no longer needed an Avatar,13]16] while Unalaq and Zaheer attempted to destroy the Avatar Spirit permanently.17]18] Korra's confidence and assertive attitude was broken when she began to question her own position as the Avatar.9]19] Despite taking a greater interest in the spiritual and diplomatic aspects of her role following Aang's restoration of her bending, the simmering complexities and pressure which preluded and followed the Water Tribe Civil War were more than she could navigate.20]19] These contributed to her lashing out, her break-up with Mako, and the dismissal of Tenzin as her spiritual guide.21]20] Korra would later mature out of this excessive combativeness.22]
Upon learning that she was kept locked away from the world by Tonraq and Tenzin, Korra became quite irate toward them, being hostile whenever they tried to speak with her.20] Korra expressed more hostility toward her father after learning about his past in the Northern Water Tribe.23] However, when she found out that Tonraq was not involved with an effort to overthrow Unalaq, she forgave him quickly.19] Korra had no qualms intimidating or threatening whoever had offended her friends and loved ones, as exemplified when she struck fear to the Equalist protester to find out Bolin's whereabouts,13] and when she threatened to feed Judge Hotah to Naga if he would not comply to her wishes. After learning the truth about her father's banishment, Korra developed an animosity toward Unalaq, which caused her to dismiss him as her spiritual mentor.7] Once she realized that Unalaq had been using her to try to free Vaatu from his prison in the Spirit World, her animosity toward her uncle increased in intensity.24] Despite this, however, Korra did lament that she could not save him from Vaatu, showing that as badly as she felt toward her uncle, she never wanted him dead.1]
After learning the origins of the Avatar Spirit through the life of Avatar Wan and regaining her memories, Korra became more patient and humble, as exemplified when she thanked Tenzin for his undying loyalty to her.22] Up until then, Korra had derived her entire self-worth from her position as the Avatar, but after witnessing Wan's life,25]26] she was able to let go of that illusion and understand that she was defined by her choices rather than her position. This epiphany would later lead her to realize that she must stop looking for a definitive right answer and instead trust her instincts. As such, she left the spirit portals open, realizing that simply maintaining the status quo did not bring balance, nor did segregating humans and spirits.1]
After the Harmonic Convergence, Korra demonstrated a more patient demeanor and took a more empathetic approach to conflict resolutions, yet still retained a sliver of her impulsive nature. This was evident when she tried to physically force Ryu, an airbending slacker, into becoming a part of the new Air Nation against his will, with Mako and Bolin eventually forced to drag Korra out to stop the mayhem.27] Nevertheless, she also showed she had become more of a pragmatic and critical thinker, being able to advise Tenzin on how to motivate the new airbenders by taking advantage of Bumi's naval background to organize them.28] With a combination of empathy and pragmatism, Korra also let Kai join the New Air Nation despite Tenzin's protest, believing the boy could change his ways through proper guidance.27]
Korra's temper and rage, once provoked, could border on murderous, as during her captivity by the Red Lotus and still believing that Zaheer had killed her father, Korra declared that she would be free and none of the Red Lotus would survive. She fully intended to kill Zaheer, showing her righteous fury, but with a dark and wrathful side to her as well.18]
After barely surviving her encounter with Zaheer and having the poison removed from her system, Korra was placed in a wheelchair during her recovery, which left her traumatized and in a state of depression. She still showed compassion and pride for her friends throughout, however, and was present when Jinora received her airbending tattoos, crying during the ceremony.18] In the following years, she struggled to recover, suffering from post-traumatic stress disorder and being haunted by flashbacks to her encounter with Zaheer. Although she recovered physically, her emotional struggle led her to embark on a journey to reconnect with her Avatar Spirit while hiding her identity as the Avatar, going against her tendency of being prideful about it.29] In spite of this, on a subconscious level, Korra was afraid of stepping back into her duties as the Avatar, terrified that if she were to do so, she would experience the same kind of trauma Zaheer incurred on her all over again.30]31] Though her anxieties of letting go of her pain impeded her recovery, after an elderly Toph helped her to face and overcome her fears of her past enemies, Korra bent the residual mercury out of her body and regained the ability to enter the Avatar State.32] However, it was not until Korra confronted Zaheer in person and was helped by him to accept what had happened to her and release her fear of "what might have been" that she was able to fully reconnect with Raava.33]
Korra's recovery increased her empathy more than ever, especially after defeating her inner darkness that she had been haunted by, to the point where she was able to empathize with her enemies such as Kuvira, whose life she saved despite her crimes. This demonstrated that she had truly become a wise and spiritually enlightened Avatar who put the needs of others before her own.29]16]34]

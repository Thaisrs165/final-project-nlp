Quick Answers
What is the significance of Draco Malfoy's blood purity belief?
Draco Malfoy was raised in a family that strongly believed in pure-blood supremacy, the idea that wizards and witches from families that haven't intermarried with Muggles or Muggle-borns are superior. This belief was a significant part of Draco's character, especially in his early years at Hogwarts. However, as he matured, Draco began to question these beliefs, showing his growth and development as a character throughout the series.

Who were Draco Malfoy's friends during his time at Hogwarts?
Draco Malfoy's constant companions throughout his years at Hogwarts were Vincent Crabbe and Gregory Goyle. Despite often treating them more like lackeys than friends, Draco showed genuine concern for them on several occasions. He also associated with other pure-blood children of his father's ex-Death Eater cronies, including Theodore Nott. However, Draco admitted that he envied the genuine friendships of Harry, Ron, and Hermione, which contrasted with his own relationships.
Provided by: Fandom
What house was Draco Malfoy sorted into at Hogwarts?
Draco Malfoy was sorted into Slytherin House at Hogwarts School of Witchcraft and Wizardry. This was in line with his family's strong belief in the importance of blood purity, a characteristic trait of Slytherin house.
Provided by: Community
Who are Draco Malfoy's parents and what is their significance in the Harry Potter series?
Draco Malfoy's parents are Lucius and Narcissa Malfoy. Lucius Malfoy was a member of the Ministry of Magic and a disgraced Death Eater during the Second Wizarding War. Narcissa Malfoy, on the other hand, showed a deep love for her family, especially Draco. Their significance in the Harry Potter series is immense. Lucius' allegiance to Lord Voldemort and his subsequent fall from grace provide a backdrop to Draco's own struggles with the dark side. Narcissa's love for Draco, meanwhile, leads her to betray Voldemort in the final book, proving instrumental in his downfall. Thus, Draco's parents play pivotal roles in shaping the series' plot and Draco's character development.

Personality and traits
— Draco taunting Harry Potter after Voldemort's return and the murder of Cedric Diggory
Draco was very capable of shutting down emotion, compartmentalising, and denying essential parts of himself. This trait is evident in his later success in Occlumency. But the denial of pain and the suppression of inner conflict only led him to be a more damaged person, who was more likely to inflict damage on others. Draco's behaviour at school was modelled on the most impressive person he knew: his father, who had been Draco's hero and role model since birth. Draco faithfully copied Lucius's cold and contemptuous manner to everyone outside his inner circle.1] He had shut down his pity, which enabled him to bully effectively.2]
Draco was, in general, an arrogant, spiteful bully for most of his early years. Like Dudley Dursley, his narcissistic nature stemmed from being raised as a pampered only son indoctrinated with his parents' beliefs.3] But unlike Dudley (who bullied physically but was not intelligent enough to know people's weak points), Draco was a 'refined type' of bully, in that he did not usually bully with physical violence but knew exactly how to hurt people verbally.4] Draco believed himself superior to most people because of his family's pure-blood lineage as well as great wealth and social standing. He frequently behaved cruelly to Muggle-borns and blood traitors, and anyone who supported Harry Potter or Albus Dumbledore. For much of his youth, Draco took after his bigoted father, Lucius Malfoy, as a role model, and frequently boasted about his family's influence and wealth.
Draco was intelligent, cunning and quick-witted, capable of deducing things and coming up with clever plans based on a small amount of information; his father had expected him to obtain top marks in school after his first year, and showed disappointment that Hermione beat him. He figured out through Montague's experience that the Vanishing Cabinets in Hogwarts and Borgin and Burkes were linked and that he could use that passage to his advantage. However, his plans could have limitations based on his own arrogance when dealing with people. When he attempted to sabotage Harry during the Gryffindor-Ravenclaw Quidditch match in their third year, his whole plan was based solely around the idea that the Dementors' appearance scared Harry, ignorant of the fact that it was the effect the Dementors had on him that caused Harry problems.
He also craved recognition and wanted to be seen as important, often boasting or performing for attention. As someone raised to believe in his own superiority, Draco was envious of Harry's fame and his Quidditch skills, and was upset when Death Eaters saw his school arch-rival Harry as such an important threat and a hated symbol but only saw Draco as a child they often met at his parents' house.1] This would also later show in his last meeting with Dumbledore, as a stressed out Draco felt comforted at receiving praise even from someone he had been raised to hate and ordered to kill.5]
Though his father always wanted Draco to have a high status and influence with the Ministry, Draco actually hoped to become a star Quidditch player (but wasn’t good enough), and mainly just wanted to be happy.6]
Draco was popular among Slytherin students, often seen surrounded by or sometimes entertaining a large crowd. Despite this, he envied Harry for his friendships with Ron and Hermione, as his relationship with his own constant companions—Crabbe and Goyle—lacked intimacy.6] That did not mean Draco did not care for his friends, as he showed genuine care for them on several occasions, and would even later risk his own life in an attempt to save them.7]
For most of his early life, Draco had moral cowardice, but was not 'wholly bad'.3] A metaphor for this was that his name came from the dragon constellation, while his wand core was of unicorn — which was symbolic of some unextinguished good at the heart of Draco.1]
The suppression of morality began to crack in Draco's sixth year at Hogwarts. After Voldemort made him a Death Eater, he gave Draco the seemingly impossible task of assassinating Albus Dumbledore. Initially, Draco shut down his compassion and suppressed virtually all the good sides of himself2]; full of fury and vengeance for his father and the desire to restore his family's status in the eyes of Voldemort, Draco barely comprehended what he was being asked to do at first, and convinced himself that he, too, wanted Dumbledore dead, as the man represented everything his father stood against.5] He became accomplished in Occlumency, capable of the Imperius Curse and the Cruciatus Curse (which he later attempted to use on Harry, who had followed Draco and discovered him crying), and set off to Hogwarts with a sense of purpose.1]
But like his first cousin once removed, Regulus Black, who had also been attracted to the Death Eater organisation, he found the reality of what it meant was way too much to handle.2] He became increasingly terrified that he would not succeed, which would result in Voldemort murdering his family, and his mission led him to abandon the things he used to enjoy: his prefect duties, playing Quidditch, and taunting Harry. He also distanced himself from his friends. After two desperate assassination attempts—that were so foolish that Dumbledore suspected Draco's heart hadn't been in it at all—in which he accidentally nearly killed two other people instead of Dumbledore, Draco's nerve began to fail. He had discovered in himself the unwillingness to kill, which he thought as a shameful failing, as he had always admired a father who advocated violence.1] Ultimately, Draco was unable to kill Dumbledore, being willing3] to take Dumbledore's offer to switch sides in exchange for the protection of his parents, and lowering his wand.5]
For the rest of the Second Wizarding War, Draco became disillusioned by the Death Eater lifestyle. He reluctantly participated in Death Eater activities and by the end of the war, remained on the fence. When Harry Potter, Ron Weasley and Hermione Granger were captured in his home, his awakened conscience led Draco to try to save Harry by not identifying him,1] and was reluctant to identify Ron and Hermione as well.7]
Although Draco tried to capture Harry again during the Battle of Hogwarts, this was a desperate attempt to save his parents' prestige and lives from Voldemort, and would've been unable to hand over Harry even if he had been successful (as he would've again found the reality of bringing about another person's death much more difficult in reality than in theory).1] As they were escaping the Room of Requirement, he risked his own life to save Goyle, and was deeply upset by Crabbe's death.7]
The events of his late teens forever changed Draco. He'd had the beliefs he'd grown up with challenged in the most frightening way: he'd experienced terror and despair, seen his parents suffer for their allegiance, and witnessed the crumbling of all his family had believed in — while the people he'd been taught to hate showed him the opposite: Dumbledore offered him help and kindness, and Harry saved his life.1]
Once the Second Wizarding War ended, Draco fully abandoned his pure-blood prejudice, refusing to follow that way of life. He did this while remaining as loving as ever to his parents, who still maintained their beliefs. He also fell deeply in love with and married Astoria Greengrass, who had gone through a similar (though less violent and frightening) conversion from pure-blood beliefs to a more tolerant life view as Draco had.1]
As a husband, Draco was very loving and devoted, standing up to his father when his parents disapproved of Astoria for failing to meet their hopes of someone from one of the Sacred Twenty-Eight. He was even fully willing to let the Malfoy bloodline end to protect her from her family's curse as it made childbirth dangerous and detrimental to her health, which he maintained even despite Lucius's dislike, only relenting after Astoria said she wanted to leave behind a child for him and not for family beliefs.
In his dual nature, Draco would keep his family's old collection of Dark artefacts in glass cases but not use them. His strange interest in alchemical manuscripts, from which he never attempted to make a Philosopher's Stone, showed his wish for something other than wealth; to be a better man.8]
His wisdom shone by the fact that even though the temptation of using his family's secret Time Turner to see his wife just for one more minute was admittedly great, his respect for her desires and memory were too great to risk jeopardising the timeline for his beloved, showing how deeply Draco had fallen in love with her, as even though he had the power to alter time to be with her, he was able to, even if barely, resist such overwhelming temptation, showing how much he had matured.
Although Scorpius's birth would cause Astoria's death, Draco's love for his son was clearly great, considering the day he was born the greatest day of his life. Having seen how his parents spoiled him had caused him to become such a horrible man in his younger days, Draco strove to do better and henceforth he raised his son to be a better person. He did not let his histories with Harry, Ron and Hermione hamper his judgement towards Scorpius's bond with Albus Potter and crush on Rose Granger-Weasley, children of his former rivals, fully supporting his son instead. His love for Scorpius was shown by how he showed no hesitation to give any Malfoy Family objects to his rivals if it would help in his son being found.
In fact, he actually trusted Albus greatly, as he allowed him to connect with Scorpius when he couldn't after Astoria died, and genuinely care for the boy for what he meant to his son. Draco even went as far as to protest to Harry for having made Albus distant from Scorpius, showing how much he valued the friendship between Albus and Scorpius. Draco was more strict with Scorpius than his parents were to him, accepting that his son must be punished for having brought the wizarding world back to the times where Voldemort was in power.
His personal relationships with his old school rivals has also changed from antagonistic to being civil and tolerating. His unpleasant behaviour towards them did somewhat resurface when he was angry at Harry for not dissolving the rumours that Scorpius was Voldemort's son just before Astoria's death, as he mocked Harry's friends for them only being so popular because of Harry's fame, and gave a cold reply letter to Ginny's offer to having Scorpius stay in the Potter House. However, he was more than willing to team up with Harry, Ginny, Ron and Hermione to save both Albus and Scorpius. He came to trust the luck and instincts of both Harry and Hermione, taking the orders of the Minister For Magic without hesitation, and acknowledging Harry was always ready to solve the thickest of situations. He eventually confessed many personal things to Harry, outright acknowledging Harry as a friend and showing respect for him, which was mutually returned by Harry.
When Scorpius was in danger, Draco showed bravery in fighting Delphini and in offering to disguise himself as Voldemort to fool her, as well as love for his son, who he refused to put in danger. He worked with his ex-rivals effectively to dispatch Delphini despite her being the daughter of Voldemort and his aunt Bellatrix, which showed that even having the Dark Lord's daughter being a blood relative was a repugnant notion to Draco, wanting little to do with his Death Eater past, further shown by how he would attend and help his rivals in defeating what remained of the forces of darkness. By this time, Draco also began referring to Voldemort by his name rather than the 'Dark Lord' as he did in his youth due to his self-redemption.
